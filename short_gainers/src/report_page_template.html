The rule search

Narrower setups worth paper-trading (found after seeing 2019–2026 data)

All rows below are earnings-free gainers that already traded above $5 the day before, which are the names you can usually borrow. The two narrow setups earn 2–2.5% per trade in both periods and stay positive at the 0.50% stress cost. They were picked after the out-of-sample results were visible, so treat them as leads to paper-trade from here on, not a proven edge.

Traps that make this trade look better than it is

1. Screening on split-adjusted prices doubles the "edge"

Price databases store prices adjusted for later splits. A penny stock that later did a 1-for-50 reverse split shows up in its old history as a "$25 stock". Screening for $5+ on those prices lets in sub-$1 pump-and-dumps you could never borrow. That roughly doubles the backtested profit. This study rebuilt the price that actually traded each day from the split history.

Holding longer: the fade keeps going, but so does the borrow bill

Covering days later instead of at the same day's close captures much more of the decline. It also means paying to borrow a stock that just spiked, and those borrow fees are often 30% to 100%+ a year. No borrow-fee history was available, so this result can't be confirmed as tradeable.

Cross-checked on a second database

The same strategy was recomputed in SQL on a separate stock database. That database only has split-adjusted prices, so the check uses the naive $5 screen on both sides. The two pipelines agree closely, month by month.

What this study can't see

  • Delisted stocks are missing. Both databases cover only companies listed today. Only of today's names have 2010 data. Spiked-then-collapsed stocks that later delisted would most likely have helped the short side, so the bias probably works against the strategy, especially for multi-day holds.
  • No borrow data. Locate availability and borrow fees for hot small caps are the real constraint, and they aren't in any free dataset. Sub-$5 results are shown only as a warning.
  • The opening print. Entry is at the official daily open. Shorting into a gap-up a few minutes later can be materially worse.
  • Many cells were examined. result cells in total, all reported. The two verdicts each rest on one pre-set cell.
  • Short-sale rules. Halts and the short-sale restriction (after a 10% drop) are not modeled.

If you want to trade it anyway

  1. Freeze the rules before you start. Core rule: the 10 biggest gainers of at least 10%, no earnings report that day or the day before, and an actual price of at least $5 both that day and the day before. Short at the next open, cover at that day's close. Track the two narrow setups (day-2 gap-up of 5% or more, RSI(14) of 85 or more) on the side. Paper-trade for at least 3–6 months. That is the only clean out-of-sample test left.
  2. Measure your real costs. Log the fill against the official open, plus locate fees and borrow availability, on every trade. The core rule only works if all-in cost stays under about 0.4% per round trip. The narrow setups have more room, about 2%.
  3. Size for the squeeze. The worst trade lost about 3× the position. At 1–2% of capital per name, a repeat costs 3–6% of the account rather than 30%.
  4. Set a kill rule in advance. For example, stop if the first 200 paper trades of the core rule average below +0.3% per trade after real costs.
